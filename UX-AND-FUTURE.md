User Experience (UX) and Conclusion

### How to use the app  
The “Safarino” app is designed with a clear and user-friendly flow. At the beginning of the experience, users can log in to make the journey feel more personal and secure. After entering the main section, users move to the search and explore stage, where they can quickly compare different travel options and results.
Next, users are guided to the destination/trip details page to review key information such as features and price. After selecting a trip, users can view and track their trips in the following sections, including the ticket/booking area and access to previous trip information. This structure helps the user always understand where they are in the process and what the next step will be.

# Advantages of the design  
1. Simplicity of the user journey: The flow is designed so users move step by step—from login to searching, browsing, and selecting—in a logical order.  
2. Fast access to important information: Key details like the destination title and price are presented clearly on different screens, helping users make decisions faster.  
3. Organized presentation of results: In the “list of trips/results” section, content is displayed using well-designed cards, making the layout neat and comparison easier.  
4. Improved post-booking tracking: By including sections related to tickets and history, users can keep track of their past bookings and follow the trip process instead of using the app only once.  
5. Consistency across the app: Since the screens were designed in XD as a unified system, the user interface looks cohesive and integrated.

# Problems faced during the design process  
During the design process, the main challenge was balancing two goals: keeping the app as complete and “perfect” as possible while also ensuring the user experience does not feel crowded or overly complex. In addition, aligning the information and transitions between different screens—such as moving from the trip list to the trip details page and then to booking/tracking—required several rounds of review to ensure the user flow stayed natural and smooth.
Another issue was finding the right balance between showing enough details and preserving the user’s visual space. If too much information is displayed, users may not be able to make decisions quickly.

# Suggestions for future development  
For future versions, it is recommended to:
- Add advanced filters in search (e.g., by price range, date, features, or trip type).  
- Improve the ticket/booking status view by showing more detailed stages (confirmed / processing / completed).  
- Expand the previous trips/history section with features like re-viewing the route, more detailed information, and recommending similar trips based on past selections.  
- Add a support or step-by-step help guide for users who may feel confused during booking or selection.  
- Conduct user testing with real users to identify weaknesses and improve specific points in the UX flow.

# Conclusion  
In the end, despite the challenges we faced in completing the flow and reaching a “near-perfect” result, the “Safarino” app design became a cohesive and presentable outcome. One of the most important achievements of this project was gaining practical experience in UX design and learning how to translate real user needs into a structured and smooth experience through different screens. Additionally, good teamwork and clear task division helped the project progress well, and we learned valuable skills together.
